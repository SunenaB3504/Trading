## A. Executive summary

**Classical Jyotiṣa has a real, rule-based price literature, but it concerns grain, oils, metals and luxury goods in an agrarian economy, not securities.** The core is Varahamihira's *Bṛhat Saṃhitā* (6th c. CE): Ch. 41 *Dravya-niścaya* (assigning commodities to zodiac signs, with house-position rules for abundance or scarcity), Ch. 42 *arghakāṇḍa* (price "fluctuation" triggered by portents at New or Full Moon, with a Sun-sign table of what to store and how many months to hold it), Ch. 40 *sasya-jātaka* (crop "horoscopes" cast at solar ingresses), Ch. 8 (Jupiter's 12- and 60-year cycles with grain-price statements), and Ch. 16 *graha-bhakti* (planets ruling commodities) ([wisdomlib TOC](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brihat-samhita)). These rules are **close to executable**: their inputs are sidereal planetary positions, lunar day-type and a benefic/malefic classification. Their outputs are directional ("flourish" vs. "scarce/dear") and categorical, never magnitudes.

**Nearly everything practitioners apply to the Sensex, Nifty or individual shares is a modern transfer [M].** Sarvatobhadra, Kota and Latta come from war, siege, muhurta and personal-prognosis contexts. Gochara (transit), daśā and Aṣṭakavarga are natal techniques that need a "birth chart" for an entity, and no exchange or index has an officially documented birth *time*. The documented market-astrology literature is mostly post-2000 English-language books from one publisher (Sagar Publications), and no audited, timestamped track record exists for any Indian market astrologer.

**The empirical record gives no support for a planetary mechanism, but it does document a behavioural one.** Belief-driven effects are documented: Rahukalam liquidity on the NSE, Muhurat-session and Friday-Amāvāsyā returns, eclipse days, Mercury retrograde. These are best explained by superstitious traders changing their behaviour. Lunar-phase return gaps (about 3–5% a year in one 48-country study) are fragile across markets and periods.

**Four obstacles stand before any rule can be tested.** Each must be fixed in advance:

- **Unresolved translation inversions.** *[Superseded: see Addendum A1, A2.]* Iyer's Ch. 41 v.9 has Jupiter in good houses making goods "perish". Ch. 42 v.14 has benefics raising prices.
- **Underspecified "strength" (*bala*).**
- **Arbitrary entity birth moments.**
- **Convention choices.** Ayanāṃśa alone moves every sidereal longitude by up to about 2.3°.

Given the multiplicity of the rule space, a few nominally "significant" results are expected by chance. The defensible design pre-registers a small number of rules, in two translation variants, against placebo ephemerides with a multiple-testing correction.

**Uncertainty.** None of the notes' authors read the Sanskrit or Bhat's translation of Ch. 40–42. Several verse loci for chakras (Phaladeepika XXVI, Muhurta Chintamani) rest on secondary websites. Several empirical papers were read only at abstract level. Everything so marked stays [V].

## C. Technique catalogue

### Part I — Classical Saṃhitā price and supply rules

These rules come from classical texts, though some readings remain [V].

#### T1. Sign-basket abundance and scarcity **[CORRECTED: see Addendum A1]** (*Dravya-niścaya*, BS Ch. 41)

1. **Name.** *Dravya-niścaya*, "determination of commodities".
2. **Source.** BS 41.1–13 [C] ([wisdomlib Ch. 41](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brihat-samhita/d/doc229186.html)). v.1 states a forecasting aim: to know "before-hand what articles can be had in abundance and what will become rare".
3. **Inputs.** Sidereal sign of Jupiter, Mercury and Venus, plus each malefic (*pāpa graha*: Sun, Mars, Saturn and the nodes, by convention [V]). A "strength" flag for malefics and benefics. Aspects. The sign → commodity table (v.2–8).
4. **Procedure.**
   - For each sign S (1–12), compute each planet's whole-sign house count h = ((sign(planet) − S) mod 12) + 1.
   - Score S under the v.9 rules, as given in Iyer's translation:
     - Jupiter in h ∈ {2,4,5,7,9,10,11} → "perish"; otherwise "flourish".
     - Mercury in h ∈ {2,5,8,10,11} → perish; otherwise flourish.
     - Venus in h ∈ {6,7} → perish; otherwise flourish.
     - Malefics in h ∈ {3,6,10,11} → flourish; otherwise the reverse.
   - Apply v.11: a *powerful* malefic outside {3,6,10,11} → goods "sell dear and become scarce".
   - Apply v.12: strong benefics in their own signs → goods flourish and prices are "moderate".
   - Apply v.13: benefic aspect protects S; aspect by a powerful malefic makes S's goods "perish".
   - Map S to its basket. For example:
     - Meṣa: cotton, wool, wheat, barley, gold.
     - Tulā: black gram, barley, wheat, mustard.
     - Vṛścika: sugarcane, metals.
     - Mīna: oils, pearls, fish.
5. **Worked example.** Jupiter in Karka (sign 4).
   - For S = Meṣa (1), h = 4 → "perish".
   - For S = Mithuna (3), h = 2 → perish.
   - For S = Karka (4), h = 1 → flourish.
   - For S = Vṛścika (8), h = ((4−8) mod 12)+1 = 9 → perish.
   - Under the Iyer-literal reading, Jupiter favours a basket only from houses 1, 3, 6, 8 and 12. That is the reverse of the usual benefic logic and is the inversion flagged below.
6. **Claimed meaning.** The sign's commodities become abundant or plentiful ("flourish", prices moderate) versus scarce or dear ("perish" / "sell dear").
7. **Variants and disagreements.**
   - [V] The Jupiter "perish" in kendra and trikoṇa houses (angular and trinal houses) may be a translation or textual inversion. It conflicts in spirit with v.12.
   - "Perish" might mean "become scarce and dear" rather than "become cheap".
   - The commodity lists differ from Ch. 16 and Ch. 42 (gold appears under the Sun in Ch. 16, under Meṣa and Makara in Ch. 41, and in a Siṃha storage rule in Ch. 42).
   - "Powerful" is undefined.
   - Tie-breaking between conflicting planets is not given ([notes on Ch. 41](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brihat-samhita/d/doc229186.html)).
8. **Testability.** [H] Fully computable from an ephemeris once these are pre-registered:
   - the benefic/malefic set;
   - a strength proxy (or ignoring it);
   - an aggregation rule (e.g., net score = flourish votes − perish votes);
   - a modern commodity map (e.g., Meṣa → wheat and gold).

   Encode **two variants**: Iyer-literal and "inverted / Sanskrit-checked".

#### T2. Syzygy portent price trigger **[CORRECTED: see Addendum A2, A14]** and Sun-sign holding table (*arghakāṇḍa*, BS Ch. 42)

1. **Name.** *Argha-kāṇḍa*, "section on prices" (Iyer: "Fluctuation of prices").
2. **Source.** BS 42.1–14 [C] ([wisdomlib Ch. 42](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brihat-samhita/d/doc229187.html)).
3. **Inputs.**
   - **Observed:** portents on *amāvāsyā* (New Moon) or *pūrṇimā* (Full Moon), namely excessive rain, meteors, *daṇḍa*, halos, eclipse or parhelion.
   - **Computed:** the Sun's sidereal sign at that time; the conjunction or aspect of benefics with the New or Full Moon; the conjunction or aspect of malefics with the Sun.
   - v.13 additionally needs a planetary-friendship table (*atimitra*, "great friend"), which is not given in the notes [V].
4. **Procedure.**
   - (a) If a portent occurs on a New or Full Moon day, prices "will rise" (v.1). The same portents on other days signal war, not prices.
   - (b) Look up the Sun's sign to select the goods and the holding period (v.2–12):

     | Sun's sign | Goods | Sell |
     |---|---|---|
     | Vṛṣabha | Wild roots and fruits | 4th month |
     | Mithuna | Juicy articles, grains | 6th month |
     | Karka | Honey, perfumes, oil, ghee, sugar | 2nd month, "when the value doubles". Earlier or later gives a loss |
     | Siṃha | Gold, gems, silver, pearls, leather, weapons | 5th month |
     | Kanyā | Chowries, asses, camels, horses | 6th month |
     | Tulā | Yarn goods, crystal, grains | 6th month |
     | Vṛścika | Durable fruits, roots, gems | After two years |
     | Dhanus | Conch, coral, pearls | 6th month |
     | Makara, Kumbha | Iron, metals, vessels, grains | End of one month |
     | Mīna | Roots, fruits, vessels, gems | 6th month |
     | Meṣa | Śarat (autumn) produce | Not stated |

   - (c) v.13: when the Sun or Moon shares a sign with an *atimitra* planet, dealing in that sign's goods is profitable.
   - (d) v.14 (Iyer summary): a New or Full Moon joined or aspected by benefics → prices rise. The Sun joined or aspected by malefics → prices fall. "The strength of the signs must be weighed."
5. **Worked example.** A lunar eclipse at *pūrṇimā* with the Sun in sidereal Siṃha.
   - Rule (a) fires.
   - Rule (b) selects gold, silver and pearls, with exit in the 5th month.
   - Testable form: the gold price change from the eclipse date to +5 months, compared with the unconditional 5-month change.
6. **Claimed meaning.** A price **rise** in the selected basket over the stated horizon. "Value doubles" is rhetorical; there is no magnitude model.
7. **Variants and disagreements.**
   - [V] The direction in v.14 (benefics → rise) looks counter-intuitive next to Ch. 8, where good years bring cheap grain. "Rise" may mean value or demand rather than nominal price.
   - v.5 is missing on wisdomlib.
   - Verbatim v.14 wording was not obtained.
8. **Testability.** [H]
   - Only the **eclipse** portent is computable. Every solar and lunar eclipse falls on an *amāvāsyā* or *pūrṇimā* by astronomical necessity, so the eclipse-only proxy reduces to "every eclipse".
   - Other portents need historical records. Excessive rain could be proxied with rainfall-station data. Meteors, halos and parhelia have no systematic historical record.
   - Small sample: roughly 4–7 eclipses a year, split across 11 Sun-sign baskets.

#### T4. **[CORRECTED: see Addendum A4]** Jupiter-year and 60-year *bārhaspatya* cycle (BS Ch. 8)

1. **Name.** *Bṛhaspati-cāra* (course of Jupiter). *Saṃvatsara* (named year of the cycle).
2. **Source.** BS 8.1, 4–6, 10, 13–14, 15–27, 40, 52 [C] ([wisdomlib Ch. 8](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brihat-samhita/d/doc226724.html)). The year system is also described at [Wikipedia: Samvatsara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samvatsara).

     - Āśvayuja year: abundant food (v.14).

#### T6. **[see Addendum A6]** Rain-yogas: Rohiṇī-yoga and Svāti-yoga (BS Ch. 24–25)

1. **Name.** *Rohiṇī-yoga*; *Svāti-yoga* (also *Āṣāḍhī-yoga*, Ch. 26, not fetched).
2. **Source.** BS 24.4–34; 25.1–6 [C] ([Ch. 24](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brihat-samhita/d/doc228925.html); [Ch. 25](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brihat-samhita/d/doc228926.html)).
3. **Inputs.**

   - *Yuddha Jayārṇava*, three fort levels plus a central *garbha* [C, V: AI translation] ([SourceLibrary p.146](https://sourcelibrary.org/book/yuddha-jayarnava/page-number/146)).
   - K. K. Joshi (ed. K. N. Rao) [M] ([Vedic Books](https://www.vedicbooks.net/astrological-application-kota-chakra-sanghatta-chakra-p-16726.html)).
   - Batra's market application [M] ([Vedams](https://www.vedamsbooks.com/books/stock_market_analysis_through_kota_chakra)).
3. **Inputs.** The asset's *janma nakṣatra* (birth star) from a chosen birth moment; transiting nakṣatras; retrograde flags.
4. **Procedure.** [V] This layout is practitioner convention with no fetched source:
   - Arrange 28 nakṣatras from the janma nakṣatra in four groups of 7.
   - Within each group the order is Bāhya → Prākāra → Madhya → Stambha (entering), then Madhya → Prākāra → Bāhya (exiting). That gives 4 Stambha, 8 Madhya, 8 Prākāra and 8 Bāhya cells.
   - Placement rules from *Yuddha Jayārṇava*:
     - benefics in the middle → growth and victory;
     - malefics in the middle → loss;
     - a malefic in the *garbha* → destruction;
     - malefics in both centre and outer zones → peril.
5. **Worked example.** Janma nakṣatra Aśvinī. Under the [V] layout, Rohiṇī (4th) is a Stambha cell. Saturn transiting forward into Rohiṇī is read as maximum "siege".
6. **Claimed meaning.** [M] The asset is the "fort": malefics entering the inner zones mean drawdown or distress. No source makes this transfer explicitly [X].
7. **Variants.** Abhijit placement; entering vs. exiting set by motion or by retrograde status; Koṭa Pāla tables. A Scribd note offers alternative rules [V] ([Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/899519944/Kota-chakra-DTP)).
8. **Testability.** [H] Daily count of malefics in Stambha/Madhya minus benefics there. It depends on an unverified layout and an arbitrary birth star.

#### T13. Graha Latta **[CORRECTED: see Addendum A10]** (planetary "kick")

1. **Name.** *Lattā*, kick. *Purolattā* is the forward kick; *Pṛṣṭhalattā* the backward kick.
2. **Source.** Cited as Phaladeepika XXVI.42–47 [V] ([PanchangBodh Latta](https://panchangbodh.com/latta-dosha-calculator)). The muhurta (beginnings, marriage) use is classical. Market use is [M].
3. **Inputs.** Each planet's nakṣatra on the 27-nakṣatra scheme (no Abhijit). All inputs are computed.
4. **Procedure.**
   - Forward kicks:
     - Sun → 12th; Mars → 3rd; Jupiter → 6th; Saturn → 8th.
     - Formula: struck = ((n−1)+(k−1)) mod 27 + 1.
   - Backward kicks:
     - Moon → 22nd; Mercury → 7th; Venus → 5th; Rāhu → 9th.
     - Formula: struck = ((n−1)−(k−1)) mod 27 + 1.
   - The struck nakṣatra stays afflicted while the kicking planet remains in its nakṣatra.
5. **Worked example.** Sun in Mūla (19): (18+11) mod 27 + 1 = 3, which is Kṛttikā. This matches the source's example. Mars in Aśvinī (1): forward 3rd = Kṛttikā.
6. **Claimed meaning.** Avoid beginnings on a nakṣatra under latta. The market extrapolation (do not open trades when the day's Moon-nakṣatra is under latta) is [M].
7. **Variants.** Ketu gets no latta classically, but some give it Rāhu's 9th backward. Retrograde motion is not addressed.
8. **Testability.** [H] A binary daily or intraday flag, fully deterministic.

### Part IV — Strength modifiers and wealth points

#### T14. Bālādi avasthā (age-states)

1. **Name.** *Bālādi avasthā*: Bāla (infant), Kumāra (youth), Yuva (adult), Vṛddha (old), Mṛta (dead).
2. **Source.** Attributed to BPHS ch. 45 [V] ([Dekho Panchang](https://dekhopanchang.com/en/learn/avasthas); [PanchangBodh](https://panchangbodh.com/baladi-avastha-calculator)). Market use [M]/[X].
3. **Inputs.** The planet's degree within its sign; whether the sign is odd or even.
4. **Procedure.** Odd signs: 0–6° Bāla, 6–12° Kumāra, 12–18° Yuva, 18–24° Vṛddha, 24–30° Mṛta. Even signs: the order is reversed. One source gives weights of 25%, 50%, 100%, "very little" and nil [V] ([vijayajyoti](https://vijayajyoti.com/?p=422)).
5. **Worked example.** Jupiter at 14° Karka (even sign) → Yuva (full strength). At 3° Karka → Mṛta.
6. **Claimed meaning.** A multiplier on another signal (a Mṛta Jupiter weakens a "bullish" signal) [M].
7. **Variants.** Do not mix with the Jāgrādi, Lajjitādi or Śayanādi schemes ([Wikipedia: Avastha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avastha_(Hindu_astrology))).
8. **Testability.** [H] Usable only as a pre-registered weight inside a composite. On its own it adds degrees of freedom.

#### T15. Indu Lagna **[SOURCE CORRECTED: see Addendum A11]** (wealth ascendant)

1. **Name.** *Indu Lagna*, the "Moon ascendant".
2. **Source.** BPHS or Phaladeepika attribution unverified [M/V] ([Astrobix](https://astrobix.com/learn/436-analysis-of-indu-lagna.html); [Vaya](https://vaya.so/blog/indu-lagna-il)).
3. **Inputs.** The lagna (ascendant) and Moon sign of the entity chart. Kalā (ray) values: Sun 30, Moon 16, Mars 6, Mercury 8, Jupiter 10, Venus 12, Saturn 1.
4. **Procedure.** Add the kalā of the 9th lord from the lagna to the kalā of the 9th lord from the Moon sign. Divide by 12 and take the remainder r (0 counts as 12). Count r signs from the Moon sign.
5. **Worked example.** Libra lagna: the 9th lord is Mercury (8). Suppose the Moon sign's 9th lord is Jupiter (10). Total 18; 18 mod 12 = 6; count 6 signs from the Moon.
6. **Claimed meaning.** Planets in or transiting the Indu Lagna (and its kendras) signify prosperity [M].
7. **Variants.** Depends wholly on an exact listing time and place.
8. **Testability.** [H] Deterministic, but only as reliable as the entity's birth moment.

### Part V — Clock-based intraday timing

#### T16. **[see Addendum A12]** Horā (planetary hour)

1. **Name.** *Horā*.
2. **Source.** [M/V] Convention; no fetched classical source. Used by Banerjee for intraday trading ([Exotic India](https://www.exoticindia.com/book/details/stock-market-astrology-astrological-theory-of-business-cycles-hau194/)).
3. **Inputs.** Local sunrise and sunset; the weekday.
4. **Procedure.** The first horā after sunrise belongs to the weekday lord. Later horās follow the descending Chaldean order: Saturn → Jupiter → Mars → Sun → Venus → Mercury → Moon. Horās are either equal (60 min) or unequal (day length ÷ 12).
5. **Worked example.** Wednesday, with sunrise taken as 06:00 and equal horās:
   - 06–07 Mercury, 07–08 Moon, 08–09 Saturn;
   - 09–10 Jupiter, 10–11 Mars, 11–12 Sun, 12–13 Venus, 13–14 Mercury, 14–15 Moon, 15–16 Saturn.
   - The NSE session (09:15–15:30) therefore spans the Jupiter through Saturn horās.
6. **Claimed meaning.** Trading in a Jupiter or Venus horā is favourable [M]/[X].
7. **Variants.** Equal vs. unequal horās; the sunrise definition; the location.
8. **Testability.** [H] An exact intraday partition. Many observations (about 6–7 per session).

#### T17. **[NOT FOUND LOCALLY: see Addendum A12]** Caughaḍiyā

1. **Name.** *Caughaḍiyā*, the "four-ghaṭikā" slot.
2. **Source.** [M] A regional Gujarati and North Indian convention. No classical citation found ([Dekho Panchang](https://dekhopanchang.com/en/learn/choghadiya); [Haindavam](https://haindavam.org/choghadiya_calculator)).
3. **Inputs.** Sunrise, sunset and the weekday.
4. **Procedure.**
   - Divide the day into 8 equal slots.
   - Slot 1 by weekday: Sunday Udvega, Monday Amṛta, Tuesday Roga, Wednesday Lābha, Thursday Śubha, Friday Cara, Saturday Kāla.
   - The sequence then cycles Udvega → Cara → Lābha → Amṛta → Kāla → Śubha → Roga. The 8th slot repeats the 1st.
   - Good: Amṛta, Śubha, Lābha, Cara (Cara sometimes neutral). Bad: Roga, Kāla, Udvega.
5. **Worked example.** Wednesday, 06:00–18:00 day, 90-minute slots:
   - Lābha 06:00, Amṛta 07:30, **Kāla 09:00**, Śubha 10:30;
   - **Roga 12:00**, **Udvega 13:30**, Cara 15:00, Lābha 16:30.
6. **Claimed meaning.** Good slots for buying and starting work [M].
7. **Variants.** The night sequence is disputed ([Swaminarayan.org](https://www.swaminarayan.org/calendar/2008/choghadiya.htm)).
8. **Testability.** [H] Deterministic, with high n.

#### T18. Rāhukāla (behavioural-channel control)

1. **Name.** *Rāhukāla*, a daily inauspicious window of about 90 minutes.
2. **Source.** Used as a belief variable by Kadiyala ([AEA 2020 paper](http://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2020/preliminary/paper/y4zD5S4S)). Its classical locus is not established in the notes [V].
3. **Inputs.** Sunrise, sunset, weekday and location.
4. **Procedure.** The commonly used segment table (day divided into 8 parts; Monday 2nd, Saturday 3rd, Friday 4th, Wednesday 5th, Thursday 6th, Tuesday 7th, Sunday 8th) is **not in the notes**. Verify it before use [V].
5. **Worked example.** Under that table, Wednesday with a 06:00–18:00 day → 12:00–13:30.
6. **Claimed meaning.** Commerce is inauspicious. Empirically, NSE liquidity and price discovery are worse during the window.
7. **Variants.** Location (the window varies with the city).
8. **Testability.** [H] The best-documented belief-channel variable for India.

#### T19. Svarodaya nominal schedule **[CORRECTED: see Addendum A13]**

1. **Name.** *Svarodaya*, "rising of the breath".
   - Channels: *Iḍā* (left, lunar), *Piṅgalā* (right, solar), *Suṣumnā* (both, central).
   - *Tattva*: element.
2. **Source.** *Śiva Svarodaya* (Rai translation) [C] ([ambuda.org](https://ambuda.org/proofing/shivsvrody-sivasvarodaya/download/text)).
   - Trade, grain collection and land under Iḍā: vv. 102–110.
   - Cattle sale-purchase under Piṅgalā: v.115. Gambling under Piṅgalā: v.116.
   - Earth gives success, water immediate profit, air and fire loss: v.163.
   - Verse numbering may differ between editions [V].
3. **Inputs.**
   - **Computed:** tithi; weekday; sunrise.
   - **Observed (the true input):** which nostril is actually flowing, and the tattva.
4. **Procedure.**
   - Expected sunrise nostril: in the bright fortnight, tithis 1–3, 7–9 and 13–15 → left; tithis 4–6 and 10–12 → right (vv. 62–64). The dark-fortnight rule is disputed [V].
   - The nostrils alternate about every 2½ ghaṭikās (v.63). [V] The translator's introduction says "2 ghaṭikās (one hour)".
   - Tattva order within each period: Vāyu, Agni, Pṛthvī, Jala, Ākāśa (v.71).
   - Nominal durations (translator's introduction, [M]): Pṛthvī ≈20 min, Jala ≈16, Agni ≈12, Vāyu ≈8, Ākāśa ≈4.
5. **Worked example.** Śukla Pañcamī: the expected flow is right for 0–60 min after sunrise, then left for 60–120 min. Within each period the nominal sequence is Vāyu 8 → Agni 12 → Pṛthvī 20 → Jala 16 → Ākāśa 4 min [V: this combines two sources].
6. **Claimed meaning.** Trade on Earth or Water under the correct svara [C for activity rules]. Price-direction claims are [X].
7. **Variants.** The dark-fortnight start; the ghaṭikā length.
8. **Testability.** [H] Only the nominal schedule can be backtested. The observed-breath version needs a prospective human study.

### Part VI — Lunar, eclipse and retrograde variables

#### T20. Lunar phase and *pakṣa* (fortnight)

1. **Name.** *Śukla pakṣa* (bright fortnight) vs. *kṛṣṇa pakṣa* (dark fortnight); *amāvāsyā* (new moon); *pūrṇimā* (full moon).
2. **Source.**
   - Classical: syzygies as Ch. 42 triggers [C].
   - The finance literature tests a mood channel ([Yuan et al.](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/yuan/papers/lunar.pdf); [Sivakumar & Sathyanarayanan](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/ijicbm/v2y2009i3p281-294.html)).
   - The Friday-Amāvāsyā effect is cultural-behavioural ([IIMB, Ghalke 2023](https://www.iimb.ac.in/imr/previous-issues/new-moon-june-2023.php)) [V].
3. **Inputs.** The Sun–Moon elongation (tithi = elongation ÷ 12°).
4. **Procedure.** Classify each trading day by pakṣa, or by the ±7-day window around new vs. full moon.
5. **Worked example.** A day with tithi 30 (amāvāsyā) falling on a Friday is flagged "Friday-Amāvāsyā".
6. **Claimed meaning.** Modern: new-moon windows give higher returns than full-moon windows. Indian cultural belief: abstaining from trade on amāvāsyā.
7. **Variants.** Tithi at sunrise vs. at the close; ±7 vs. ±3-day windows.
8. **Testability.** [H] High n. Prior literature exists, so this is a replication, not a discovery.

#### T21. Eclipses

1. **Name.** *Grahaṇa* (eclipse). Rāhu-cāra (BS ch. 5, attribution [V]).
2. **Source.** BS 42.1, eclipse as a syzygy portent → price rise [C]. Lepori's US study [V] ([Marginal Revolution summary](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2009/07/does-superstition-matter-for-the-stock-market.html)). Vasudev's eclipse use [M].
3. **Inputs.** The eclipse time and type from an ephemeris; whether it is visible from India (optional).
4. **Procedure.** See T2 for the classical trigger. The behavioural version takes the return on the eclipse day and the following 1–5 days.
5. **Worked example.** See T2.
6. **Claimed meaning.** Classical: the basket price rises. Behavioural: low return and volume on the day, then reversal (about 10 bp a day in US data).
7. **Variants.** Only eclipses visible locally; solar vs. lunar.
8. **Testability.** [H] n ≈ 4–7 a year.

#### T22. Mercury retrograde and combustion (*vakra*, *asta*)

1. **Name.** *Vakra* (retrograde); *asta* (combust).
2. **Source.** [M] Western-hybrid and popular use. Classical texts treat retrograde motion as strength (*ceṣṭā bala*) with school-dependent readings [V].
3. **Inputs.** The planet's speed sign; its angular distance from the Sun.
4. **Procedure.** Flag the retrograde period. Combustion orbs commonly quoted [V]: Moon 12°, Mars 17°, Mercury 14° (12° retrograde), Jupiter 11°, Venus 10° (8° retrograde), Saturn 15°.
5. **Worked example.** On any day Mercury's geocentric speed is below 0, the flag = 1.
6. **Claimed meaning.** Popular: confusion and losses.
7. **Variants.** Shadow periods; orb sets.
8. **Testability.** [H] The empirical sign conflicts between studies: global −3.2% a year vs. India positive ([Wang & Zhang](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/576994/Hang-Wang-Hermes2021.pdf); [Mahendra et al. 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/apfinm/v28y2021i1d10.1007_s10690-020-09310-8.html)). That makes it a good out-of-sample test.

## D. Convention matrix

The choices that most change outputs are the **entity moment** and the **ayanāṃśa**.

- **Ayanāṃśa.** The J2000 values are Lahiri 23.857°, KP 23.760°, Raman 22.411° and Fagan-Bradley 24.740°. So Lahiri − Raman ≈ 1.45° and FB − Lahiri ≈ 0.88° ([kerykeion](https://kerykeion.net/libephemeris/docs/reference/ayanamsha); [Swiss Ephemeris](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swisseph.htm)). That is enough to move a planet across a nakṣatra-pāda boundary (3°20′ wide), a KP sub boundary (under 1°) or a sign cusp.
- **Ephemeris.** Swiss Ephemeris ≥ v2.00 is built on JPL DE431 and matches it to 0.001″, so ephemeris choice is negligible. Its licence is AGPL or a paid professional licence ([Swiss Ephemeris](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swisseph.htm)).
- **Sunrise.** Swiss Ephemeris offers a Hindu-rising flag, `SE_BIT_HINDU_RISING` ([swephprg](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swephprg.htm)). The minutes of difference between sunrise definitions can flip a vāra or tithi at the day boundary.
- **Nifty dates.** The official factsheet gives the **base date as 3 Nov 1995 (value 1000)** and the **launch as 22 Apr 1996** ([NSE factsheet](https://www.niftyindices.com/Factsheet//ind_nifty50.pdf)). The switch to free-float on 26 Jun 2009 is a candidate "re-birth" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NIFTY_50)).
- **Other entity dates.** No official birth *time* exists for India, the BSE, the NSE, the Sensex or the Nifty. BSE founding (1875), Sensex base 1978-79 = 100, and NSE incorporation (Nov 1992) are only partly dated, by day or by month [V] ([edinformatics](https://www.edinformatics.com/investor_education/bse30.htm); [StockEdge](https://blog.stockedge.com/commemorating-30-years-of-nse/)).
- **India's national chart.** It is cast with a Taurus lagna at 00:00 15 Aug 1947 ([Tempora](https://www.tempora.ltd/findings/india-national-chart)). A Cancer lagna asserted elsewhere [V/X] would imply a different event moment, because the Sun is in Cancer and a Cancer lagna requires a time near sunrise.

## E. Testability assessment

All astronomical inputs can be computed in advance from an ephemeris. **Ephemeris look-ahead is therefore not the risk. The risk is researcher look-ahead**: choosing the entity chart, school, translation or modern commodity map after seeing price history.

**Cross-cutting failure modes.**

- **Multiplicity.** Planets × signs × houses × nakṣatras × schools × lags × horizons give thousands of rules. Calendar-rule studies show that individually "extremely significant" rules lose significance against the full universe tested ([Sullivan, Timmermann & White](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cdl:ucsdec:qt2z02z6d9)).
- **Small samples.** Annual and seasonal rules (T3, T4, T7, T9) cannot reach meaningful power within the Nifty era.
- **Overlapping windows.** T2's multi-month holds need HAC or block-bootstrap inference.
- **Confounds.** Indian festivals cluster near holidays, expiry days, budget and RBI policy dates, and the Diwali season ([Floros & Tan](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/emffin/v12y2013i1p107-127.html) show that lunar effects are entangled with Monday and January effects).
- **Commodity data.** Pre-modern grain series face changes in index composition, price controls and procurement policy.

## F. Empirical literature review

### Lunar-phase effects are directionally consistent but fragile

The two most-cited studies agree on direction. **Dichev and Janes** report that returns in the 15 days around new moons are roughly double those around full moons, across about 100 years of US data and 24 other countries, with no lunar effect on volatility or volume ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/lunar-cycle-and-stock-returns/)). Its publication venue and year (2001 working paper, cited as 2003) remain [V]. **Yuan, Zheng and Zhu (2006)** find returns **3–5% a year lower around full moons** across 48 countries, not explained by volatility, volume, announcements or other calendar anomalies ([LSE](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/39409)). Dichev himself said, "I don't think you can make riches trading on this" ([CFO.com](https://www.cfo.com/news/bad-moon-rising/675658/)).

Broader samples weaken the result. **Floros and Tan (2013)** test 59 markets with TGARCH. They find full-moon effects in only 6 markets and new-moon effects in 8, "strongly influenced" by Monday and January effects ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/emffin/v12y2013i1p107-127.html)). A non-peer-reviewed S&P 500 re-test for 1990–2018 found the new-moon edge small relative to volatility and unstable across subperiods ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/lunar-cycle-and-stock-returns/)). Indian evidence conflicts. **Sivakumar and Sathyanarayanan (2009)** find the śukla vs. kṛṣṇa pakṣa effect on the Sensex "quite limited" over about 17 years ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/ijicbm/v2y2009i3p281-294.html)). A 2022 claim of a fourfold new-moon advantage could not be verified [V].

### Superstition moves prices through believers, not planets

The strongest-identified studies all point to a **belief channel**:

- **Hirshleifer, Jian and Zhang** (*Management Science*). Chinese IPOs with lucky listing codes trade at an initial premium and then underperform, with the premium gone within about three years ([SSRN](https://dx.doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.1460522)).
- **Wang and Zhang.** Returns are about **3.2–3.3% a year lower during Mercury retrograde** across 48 countries. Astrological proxies (traffic accidents, firm news) showed no change, which points to believer withdrawal rather than any astral mechanism ([working paper](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/576994/Hang-Wang-Hermes2021.pdf)). Journal publication is unconfirmed [V].
- **India, opposite sign.** Mahendra, Mohanty and Sudalaimuthu (2021) report a *positive* Mercury-retrograde effect on the Nifty and Sensex for 1998–2018 using EGARCH, so the sign is opposite to the global result ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/apfinm/v28y2021i1d10.1007_s10690-020-09310-8.html)).
- **Lepori.** 362 eclipses from 1928 to 2008 show below-average returns and volume on eclipse days, followed by a reversal of about 10 bp a day. The effect is larger when media coverage is heavy ([Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2009/07/does-superstition-matter-for-the-stock-market.html)) [V].

Indian studies follow the same belief-channel pattern:

- **Kadiyala (AEA 2020).** Across 1,786 NSE stocks, liquidity and price discovery **deteriorate during Rāhukāla**. Nifty 50 membership and higher volume cushion the effect ([AEA](http://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2020/preliminary/paper/y4zD5S4S)).
- **Ghalke (2023).** Significantly negative returns on Friday amāvāsyā days and positive returns on Muhurat-trading days, both stronger on the retail-heavy BSE ([IIMB](https://www.iimb.ac.in/imr/previous-issues/new-moon-june-2023.php)) [V: abstract only].
- **Muhurat sessions.** These average about +0.53%, with 8 of 10 positive over a decade ([Groww](https://groww.in/blog/muhurat-trading-over-the-last-decade)). But n ≈ 1 a year, and the session is a ceremonial, low-volume hour.
- **Chinese evidence.** A review finds superstition-linked mispricing concentrated where unsophisticated investors dominate ([CAFR](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/cafr-01-2023-0001/full/html)).

**No peer-reviewed, out-of-sample test of any Jyotiṣa forecasting rule was found.** That includes daśā, gochara, SBC, Kota and the BS *argha* rules applied to NSE, BSE or commodity data. No rigorous test of the Bradley siderograph was found either.

### Astrologers perform at chance in controlled tests

- **Carlson (1985), *Nature*.** In an astrologer-agreed double-blind protocol, matching was at chance (1 in 3) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Carlson)). Ertel's 2009 reanalysis finds marginal p = .054 and calls the negative verdict "untenable", while not claiming support for astrology ([PhilPapers](https://api.philpapers.org/rec/ERTAOS)).
- **Dean and Kelly (2003).** A meta-analysis of more than 40 controlled studies found astrologers no better than chance, and "time twins" showed "no hint" of similarity ([Ingenta](https://www.ingentaconnect.com/content/imp/jcs/2003/00000010/f0020006/art00010)).
- **Narlikar–Dabholkar test of Vedic astrologers (2008, published 2009).** Of 27 astrologers who returned answers, the best score was 24/40 against a pass mark of 28 ([Kartavya Sadhana](https://kartavyasadhana.in/view-article/jayant-naralikar-on-anti-astrology-campaign-with-narendra-dabholkar)). The primary paper was not read [V].

### Data-snooping literature sets the bar

- **Harvey, Liu and Zhu.** New factors should clear **t ≥ 3** ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/taming-the-factor-zoo/)).
- **McLean and Pontiff.** Predictor returns fall **26% out of sample and 58% after publication** ([Gwern PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)).
- **Bailey and López de Prado.** The deflated Sharpe ratio corrects for the number of trials ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)).
- **Counterpoint.** Some argue p-hacking concerns are overstated ([FEDS 2019-016](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2019016pap.pdf)). That debate concerns economically motivated factors, not astrological ones.

### No audited practitioner track record exists

- Banerjee's claim to have foreseen the 1998–2001 declines in a 1995 magazine is self-reported ([Exotic India](https://www.exoticindia.com/book/details/stock-market-astrology-astrological-theory-of-business-cycles-hau194/)).
- Vasudev's Sensex and bullion analyses are ex post.
- Websites have claimed "95% success" ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/is-the-hand-of-god-playing-around-dalal-street/5273.html)).
- A rationalist offer of Rs 2.5 million for a correct one-week market prediction reportedly drew no takers ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/business%2Fmarkets%2Fastrospeak-and-stock-markets-1.639803)).
- **Regulatory context.** SEBI's 2024 finfluencer rules and the RA amendments are method-neutral. A paid astrology-based trading call plausibly falls under research-analyst regulation, but SEBI has not ruled on astrology specifically [V] ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/sebi-announces-norms-to-restrict-finance-influencer-s-recommendations-124062701002_1.html); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2024/12/19/sebi-research-analyst-third-amendment-regulations-2024/)).

## H. Gaps and open questions

*[Several items below are resolved or reframed by the local-source audit: see Addendum D.]*

### Textual and philological questions for a human Jyotiṣa or Sanskrit scholar

These are the highest priority.

- **Ch. 40–42 against the Sanskrit.** Compare Iyer's BS Ch. 40–42 with M. R. Bhat (1981–82), the Sanskrit (Kern 1865) and Bhaṭṭotpala's commentary.
  - Specifically: does 41.9 really have Jupiter in kendra and trikoṇa houses making goods "perish"?
  - Does 42.14 really have benefics *raising* prices, and does "rise" mean nominal price, value or demand?
  - Supply the missing verses 42.5 and 41.10.
  - Confirm whether Bhat's verse numbers match Iyer's.
- **Ch. 8.** Complete and verify the nakṣatra → month map, including the suspect Svāti/Bharaṇī/Revatī → Āśvayuja group.
- **Operational definitions.** Define "powerful" (*balavān*), *atimitra* (the friendship table) and the benefic/malefic status of the Moon and Mercury as Varahamihira used them.
- **Chakra sources and loci.**
  - Verify the SBC grid (cell coordinates) and choose a vedha school with a textual warrant.
  - Verify the Kota layout (Stambha/Madhya/Prākāra/Bāhya order) and the Koṭa Pāla rule from Narapatijayacaryā.
  - Confirm the Latta verse locus (Phaladeepika XXVI vs. Muhurta Chintamani) and its counts.
  - Confirm the Phaladeepika ch. 26 gochara and vedha tables.
- **Texts not consulted.** Check whether the Bhadrabāhu Saṃhitā, Meghamālā, Nārada Saṃhitā, Garga Saṃhitā, Vasantarāja Śakuna or Tājika works contain *argha* rules that differ from the BS.
- **Scholarly literature not consulted.** Pingree's *Jyotiḥśāstra* (1981), Plofker, Yano and Gansten.
- **Modern mapping.** Advise on whether any mapping of classical commodity baskets to modern instruments is defensible. For example: does the Meṣa basket "gold" map to MCX gold? Does any basket map to an equity index at all?

### Empirical and data gaps

- No pre-registered test of astrological market timing exists anywhere in the reviewed literature.
- No academic ethnography of Marwari *teji-mandi* practice. Timberg documents speculative ledgers (*fatka*) from 1791, but not astrological methods ([Moneylife review](https://moneylife.in/article/the-marwaris-book-review/37562.html)).
- No verified survey of astrology use among Indian traders.
- Official birth times for the BSE, NSE, Sensex and Nifty were not retrieved: BSE returned HTTP 403 and NSE timed out.
- Several key papers were read only at abstract level: Ghalke 2023; the 2011 *JEF* lunar follow-up; the Kumar Diwali study; Narlikar's *Current Science* paper.
- The Mercury-retrograde sign conflict (global negative, India positive) is unresolved.

- **Practice of buyer-to-entity matching (T23).** No source here documents how widely practitioners use name-letter, Tārā Bala or kūṭa scoring between a person and a company. Find a primary source or a practitioner interview before describing prevalence [V].
- **Frozen name rule.** Which of legal name, trade name or ticker, in which script and spelling, defines the name-letter for H21. Choose before any data is examined.

### Design questions

- Should the classical rules be tested only on commodities, where their domain claim lies, and excluded from equity indices, which would be a category transfer? A defensible plan tests classical rules on commodities and modern rules on indices, and reports them as separate families.
- How should a practitioner be involved? Ideally they agree the rules and success thresholds in advance, as in Carlson and Narlikar, but are blinded to outcomes.

## Conclusion

The tradition's own centre of gravity is not stock-market "vibe" but **the agrarian price cycle**: will grain, oil and metals be plentiful or dear, and how long should stored goods be held? That question has a native, explicit and largely computable rule set in Varahamihira. The most faithful experiment therefore tests BS Ch. 41–42 on commodity prices. Encoding the chakras on the Nifty tests a 20th–21st-century adaptation whose key inputs (birth moment, name-letter, school) have no textual anchor. Paradoxically, the classical rules are both more legitimate and harder to test: their directional verbs are ambiguous in translation, and their signals are slow and sparse.

The second insight is that **any measurable effect is likely to be sociological, not sidereal.** Where Indian markets respond to the Jyotiṣa calendar (Rāhukāla, Muhurat sessions, amāvāsyā Fridays), the effect follows believers and fades with liquidity and institutional ownership. A well-designed experiment should therefore test the astral claim and the belief channel separately (H20). A null on the first and a positive on the second would be the most informative and the most probable outcome. Before any data are touched, the single highest-value step is to have a Sanskrit-competent Jyotiṣa scholar fix the Ch. 41–42 readings and the chakra layouts.
