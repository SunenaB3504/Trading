# Audit of the Jathakam-dasha study (IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md)

Date: 2026-10-25. Method: read the plan, the pipeline scripts in `tools/`, the git history, and re-derived the main numbers from the study's own derived files (`research/audit_2026-10/audit_jathakam.py`, output `audit_jathakam.json`). The raw NSE files are not in the repo (`data/raw/` is git-ignored), so the pipeline was not re-run from raw data. Education and research only, not investment advice.

## Short answers

1. **Hold-out split removes snooping: No, only in part.** The split itself is correct and deterministic. The study around it is not pre-registered and Cohort B was not kept clean.
2. **Log compounding and distress filter fix the penny-stock problem: Partly.** Log compounding correctly removes the volatility drag. The distress filter is applied on the outcome variable and was added after the results were seen. Neither makes the result robust.
3. **Deterministic and complete enough to verify every metric: Partly.** All 14 published hashes match the files, and the headline numbers reproduce from those files. The inputs (raw prices, benchmark) are not available or hashed, and the environment is not pinned.

## 1. Pre-registration and the 50/50 split

What holds:
- The rule `int(sha256(symbol)[:8],16) % 2` reproduces the split exactly: 412 in Cohort A, 396 in Cohort B (808 total).
- The code comment says the split is "stratified by listing decade", but the rule uses the symbol hash only. The comment is wrong; the result is unaffected.

What does not hold:
- **The plan was changed after results.** The first plan commit (`5ac7912`) has no geometric log alpha, no distress filter and no rules R-1 to R-3. R-1 to R-3 appear in `5235116` (after Cohort A results), and the log and distress suite in `98576cd`, after Cohort B results (`4c7fec7`). The header "declared before data inspection" is therefore not accurate. All commits are dated 2026-10-25 and there is no external timestamp such as OpenTimestamps.
- **Cohort B was used outside confirmation.** The robustness script (`build_robustness_analysis.py`) reads B and the pooled universe. Phases 6A (Panchanga), 6B (12 houses) and 6C ran on all 808 stocks with no hold-out, yet carry the headline claims (Mahapata yogas, Chandrashtama, House 9, +41.20% alpha).
- **Rule outcomes drifted in the write-up.** Pre-registered R-1 failed on its drawdown condition, and R-3 failed its size threshold. The text still says "replicated with tremendous power" for `[T-T-T]` alpha, which was not a pre-registered rule.
- **Many comparisons, no correction.** 8 confluence states, 9 PD lords, 12 houses, Tithi/Karana/Yoga categories. No multiplicity adjustment; the plan defers FDR to a Stage 2 that was not run.
- **Test statistics treat days as independent.** The p-values use Welch t-tests on daily returns, but returns within a stock are correlated and all stocks share the same market days. A stock-level bootstrap (below) is much wider.

## 2. Base-effect trap, log compounding and the distress filter

Re-derived from `phase3_daily_market_metrics.parquet` (the study's file), crisis days masked:

| | Cohort A [T-T-T] | Cohort A [D-D-D] | Cohort B [T-T-T] | Cohort B [D-D-D] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stock-days / stocks | 6,340 / 56 | 9,352 / 65 | 5,349 / 59 | 5,507 / 55 |
| Arithmetic alpha | +28.10% | -3.41% | +35.12% | +15.09% |
| Log alpha | +18.77% | -15.31% | +21.10% | -3.50% |
| Share of days with 30%+ drawdown | 55.6% | 70.5% | 59.4% | 55.7% |

All study figures reproduce (alpha spread A 31.51, B 20.03; drawdown spread A +14.82, B -3.74).

Stock-level bootstrap (resampling stocks, 1,000 draws), spread of [T-T-T] over [D-D-D]:
- Alpha, Cohort A: +31.5 points, 95% interval +3.9 to +60.3 (111 stocks).
- Alpha, Cohort B: +20.0 points, 95% interval **-11.9 to +50.7** (105 stocks), which includes zero.
- Drawdown rate, A: +14.8 points, interval -0.6 to +30.5. B: -3.7 points, interval -23.0 to +16.4.

So the out-of-sample alpha spread is not distinguishable from zero once stocks, not days, are the unit.

Log compounding: the gap between arithmetic and log alpha is 14.0 points (T-T-T) and 18.6 points (D-D-D) in Cohort B, which equals the expected volatility drag (daily volatility 3.4%, so sigma-squared over 2 times 252 is about 14). That part is correct and useful. Two cautions: the study logs `ln(1 + stock return minus beta-adjusted market return)`, a difference of returns and not the log of a wealth ratio; and log alpha is not zero for the favoured states, so it does not by itself remove the effect. It also does not remove bid-ask bounce in thin stocks.

Distress filter (drop days with drawdown of 75% or worse, or close below Rs 20):
- It conditions on the same drawdown variable used as an outcome (share of days with 30%+ drawdown). Cohort B [D-D-D] falls from 55.7% to 44.5% just by removing the deepest days; this is mechanical, not evidence.
- 16-17% of days in each state are removed, so the filtered result describes a different, selected set of stock-days.
- The Rs 20 rule uses the bhavcopy `close` column, which is a nominal price (not checked against split adjustment), so a good stock after a split or bonus may drop out.
- It was added after seeing the results, so it is a post-hoc choice.

Other issues that affect both questions:
- **Survivorship:** the universe comes from `EQUITY_L.csv` (currently listed). All 802 stocks with data have prices through 2026-09 or later; none was delisted. Failed companies are absent, which flatters every state.
- **Dasha state tracks stock age**; median age in B differs between [T-T-T] (3.7 years) and [D-D-D] (2.6 years). Age is not controlled, and young post-IPO stocks behave differently.
- **Chart time:** 10:00 IST is a proxy. A 45-minute shift moves the dasha boundaries by months (PH-13/14 run: the bhukti lord differs for 24% of stocks between 10:00 and 09:15 charts).
- **Compare with the repo's own tests:** the PH-13, PH-14 and PH-15 runs on a stricter design found no dasha effect on collapse or on forward returns (`docs/PH13_14_PH15_RESULTS.md`).

## 3. Determinism and completeness

Verified: all 14 SHA-256 values in section 9.3 match the files on disk (this proves the files are unchanged, not that the pipeline reproduces them). Step scripts exist for every phase.

Gaps:
- **Inputs are not available or hashed.** `data/raw/` is git-ignored; no hash of the NSE bars, IPO lists, `EQUITY_L.csv` or index files is recorded. Without them nothing can be re-run.
- **Benchmark mismatch:** the plan says Nifty 500 and VIX above 32; the code loads Nifty 50 or Nifty 500 where available and otherwise uses the equal-weight mean of the study universe, and no VIX series is used.
- **Ephemeris not pinned:** `pyswisseph` version and the Swiss Ephemeris data files are not specified, and a missing data file silently falls back to the Moshier ephemeris. The plan specifies True Node and Mumbai BKC coordinates; only the Moon enters the dasha, so the node setting does not matter, but the Lagna uses the site coordinates (the repo's other studies use 18.93 N, 72.83 E).
- **Parquet hashes** depend on pyarrow version and compression, so they may differ on another machine even when the data is identical. The plan hashes the parquet file, not its contents.
- **Hard-coded paths** (`c:\Users\Admin\Sunil\Trading`) in the robustness script.
- **Dasha function** checked: same Vimshottari order, years and 365.25-day year as this repo's engine.

## What to do next (suggested)

2. Treat all of Phase 6 as exploratory; label Cohort B as used.
3. Freeze a single new hypothesis (for example [T-T-T] minus [D-D-D] alpha, stock-level inference, age-controlled, delisted stocks added) with an external timestamp and test it on data not yet used.
4. Add delisted and failed stocks before any claim about drawdown protection.
