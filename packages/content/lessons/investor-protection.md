India has a regulator, exchange systems and complaint channels for investors. Know where they are before you need them.

| Body | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| SEBI | Check registrations; file complaints on SCORES |
| NSE and BSE | Company announcements, lot sizes, circulars |
| NSDL and CDSL | Demat statements, nominee details |
| AMFI | Mutual fund data and distributor registration (ARN) |
| RBI | Government bonds, bank rules |
| Income-tax Department | Filing returns, tax statements |

**Checks before you trust anyone.** Look up the broker's SEBI registration and exchange membership. Verify an adviser's registration number. Confirm that an app belongs to a registered broker.

**Complaint route.** Complain first to the company or broker and keep the reference. If there is no answer in the stated time, file on SEBI's SCORES portal. For fraud, also report to the police and the cyber crime helpline.

**What protection exists and what does not.** Shares in a Demat account are held in your name in the depository, not with the broker. Exchange investor protection funds give limited compensation if a member defaults. Nothing protects you from losses caused by market falls or by your own choices. Cash left with a broker is the part most at risk.

**Nominee and records.** Nominate a person for every Demat account and fund folio. Share the details with a trusted family member. Keep contract notes, capital gains reports and tax statements for at least 6 years.

**Official sources.** Rules change, so confirm any rule with the SEBI, NSE, BSE, AMFI or Income-tax Department site.
