Tax is a cost on your gains, and the rate depends on how long you held and what you traded. The rates here are as reported for 2026 and change often. Confirm them with the Income-tax Department or a tax adviser. This is for learning, not tax advice.

| Trade | Treated as | How it is taxed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shares or equity ETFs sold within 12 months | Short-term capital gain | 20% |
| Shares or equity ETFs sold after more than 12 months | Long-term capital gain | 12.5% on gains above ₹1,25,000 in the year |
| Shares bought and sold the same day | Speculative business income | Your slab rate |
| Futures and options | Non-speculative business income | Your slab rate |
| Gold and liquid ETFs | Taxed as slab-rate income | Your slab rate |

A 4% cess is added to the tax on capital gains.

**Worked example.** You make a ₹2,00,000 short-term gain and a ₹3,00,000 long-term gain in a year. Short-term tax is 20% of 2,00,000 = ₹40,000. The long-term gain above ₹1,25,000 is ₹1,75,000, taxed at 12.5% = ₹21,875. Together ₹61,875, plus 4% cess of ₹2,475, so ₹64,350.

**Losses.** A short-term loss can be set off against short-term and long-term gains. A long-term loss can be set off only against long-term gains. Unused capital losses carry forward for 8 years if the return is filed on time. Speculative losses can only be set off against speculative gains.

**Futures and options.** They are business income, so costs such as brokerage can be deducted, and the books may need an audit above certain turnover limits. Turnover is not the same as profit. Ask a tax adviser how it applies to you.

**Keep records.** Contract notes and capital gains statements for at least 6 years. The Tax screen in Practise shows how your practice trades would be classified.

**Habit.** Before you sell, check how long you have held it. A few days can change the tax rate.
