Fraud in investing usually looks like an opportunity. The common thread is a promise that is too good, from a person you do not know, with pressure to act quickly.

| Scam | Warning signs |
| --- | --- |
| Tips groups on messaging apps | Guaranteed returns, urgency, paid memberships, fake screenshots |
| Pump and dump | Sudden rise on no news and thin volume |
| Fake trading apps | Link from a stranger; app not from a registered broker |
| Unlisted IPO and pre-IPO offers | Offers outside the exchange system |
| Unregistered advisers | No registration number; claims of fixed returns |
| Ponzi schemes | Fixed high monthly returns |

**Rules that protect you.**

1. Trade only through a SEBI-registered broker and check the registration.
2. Take advice only from SEBI-registered advisers or research analysts.
3. Never send money to a personal account for a trade.
4. Never share OTPs, passwords or screen-sharing access.
5. Your shares should show in a Demat account in your name.
6. Assume a guaranteed return is false.

**Worked example: a guaranteed return fails.** A scheme promises 5% a month. Compounded, that is about 80% a year. If it could deliver that reliably, it would not need your small deposit.

**How a tip group profits.** The leader buys a thin stock, tells followers to buy, and sells into the rise. The followers are the buyers on the other side. Ask who gains if you act.

**If you have been cheated.** Stop sending money. Keep messages, transfers and screenshots. Report quickly to the cyber crime helpline 1930 and your bank. Complain to SEBI through SCORES if a registered entity is involved, and to the police. Do not pay anyone who promises to recover your money for a fee.

**Final test.** If you cannot explain in one sentence how the investment makes money, do not buy it.
