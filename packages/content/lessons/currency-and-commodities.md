A currency future is a contract on an exchange rate. The one in this app is USD/INR: how many rupees one US dollar costs. If you buy it, you gain when the dollar becomes dearer in rupees, which means the rupee gets weaker.

**The contract.** One NSE USD/INR lot is 1,000 US dollars. The smallest price step is 0.0025 rupee, so one step is worth ₹2.50 a lot. A one-rupee move in the rate changes your account by ₹1,000 a lot.

**Worked example.** The rate is 80.00. One lot is worth 1,000 x 80 = ₹80,000. You buy one lot and the rate goes to 81.50. You gain 1.50 x 1,000 = ₹1,500 before charges. If it had gone to 78.50 you would have lost ₹1,500.

**Why a quiet rate is not a safe one.** A currency moves little each day, so the margin asked is small: in this app about 3% of the contract value, which is about ₹2,400 on the lot above. Small margin makes it easy to hold many lots. Ten lots is ₹8,00,000 of exposure, and a two-rupee move against you costs ₹20,000. A run of one-way days is all it takes. Size the position by what a two-rupee move costs, not by the margin.

**Settlement.** Currency futures settle in cash in rupees. There are no dollars to deliver. They expire monthly. In this app the expiry is two working days before the last working day of the month, and the exchange calendar is the source to check. Close or roll before that day.

**Who uses them.** An importer who must pay dollars in three months can buy the future to fix the cost. An exporter does the opposite. They want to remove risk. A trader who holds a view with no dollar business is taking the risk they are removing.

**What this app models.** The rates come from a public data source, Yahoo Finance, not from the exchange, and are placed on the NSE trading days. The margin, the charges and the expiry rule are approximate, and the price step is rounded to a whole paisa. Treat the practice as a way to learn the size of the risk, not to learn exact exchange figures.

**Commodities.** Commodities are goods such as gold, silver, crude oil and farm products. Most are traded on a separate commodity exchange. This app does not model commodity futures. You can already trade gold through the gold ETF in the practice account, which behaves like a share and is taxed at the slab rate. Prices of commodities move with supply, demand and the dollar, so gold and oil often move when the rupee moves.

**Habit.** For any leveraged contract, write down the rupee cost of a bad move before you place the order.
