Markets fall hard from time to time. The Nifty record in this project's NSE data begins in September 2007 and shows two large falls.

| | 2008 | 2020 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Peak | 6,287.85 on 8 Jan 2008 | 12,224.55 on 20 Jan 2020 |
| Lowest close | 2,524.20 on 27 Oct 2008 | 7,610.25 on 23 Mar 2020 |
| Fall | 59.9% | 37.7% |
| Days from peak to low | 293 | 63 |
| Regained the old peak | 9 Nov 2010 | 6 Nov 2020 |

The 2008 fall took nearly three years from the peak to a new high. The 2020 fall healed in under ten months. The pace differs each time, so no timeline can be assumed.

**The range of years.** Calendar-year changes in the Nifty 50 price index in this data include −52% in 2008 and +71% in 2009. The long-run rise of about 8.8% a year from 2007 to 2026 hides both.

**Most stocks.** The index flatters the typical stock. Among 889 clean collapses in our data, many peaked in 2007, 2008 and 2010 and did not come back. An index recovers; a single stock may not.

**Lessons.**

1. A fall of 38% to 60% in the index has happened twice in 19 years. Plan for it.
2. Falls and recoveries are fast. The year after the 2008 low gained 92%.
3. Keep money you will not have to sell. The emergency fund stays outside shares.
4. Do not extrapolate from the latest year.

**Habit.** Test your plan against a 40% fall. Ask what you would sell, and whether you would have to.
