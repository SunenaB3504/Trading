Good outcomes mostly come from a few boring habits followed for years. None needs special knowledge.

**1. Diversify across assets and across stocks.** Hold a mix such as shares, bonds, gold and cash, and within shares hold many companies or an index fund. Diversification does not remove risk. It stops one failure from ending the plan.

**2. Decide your asset allocation, then rebalance.** Pick a split, such as 60% shares and 40% bonds, that matches your goal and your ability to bear loss. Once a year, sell what has grown too large and buy what has shrunk, to restore the split.

| Example | Start  | After shares rise 30%, bonds flat | Rebalanced |
|---------|--------|-----------------------------------|------------|
| Shares  | 60,000 | 78,000                            | 70,800     |
| Bonds   | 40,000 | 40,000                            | 47,200     |

In the example, the portfolio grew to 1,18,000. At 60/40 the shares should be 70,800 and bonds 47,200, so you move 7,200 from shares to bonds.

**3. Size every position.** Limit one stock to about 5% of your savings and one sector to about 20%. Smaller positions are easier to hold through a fall.

**4. Invest regularly.** A monthly SIP removes the guessing game and builds the habit.

**5. Keep costs low.** Direct plans, index funds, few trades. A fee of 1% a year compounds against you.

**6. Keep a decision journal.** Write why you bought, what would prove you wrong, and when you will review.

**7. Review on a schedule.** Look at your portfolio quarterly, not daily. Daily price watching raises stress and trading.

**8. Separate trading from investing.** If you must trade, use a small fixed amount you can afford to lose entirely.

**Before you buy: a checklist.**

I can explain in one sentence how this business makes money.

I have read the last annual report summary and the shareholding pattern.

Debt and cash flow look sound.

Average daily volume is high enough for me to exit.

The position is no more than 5% of my savings.

I have written what would make me sell.

**Before you sell: a checklist.**

Has the reason I bought changed?

Am I selling because of price, news or fear?

Do I have a better use for this money?

Have I counted tax and costs?
