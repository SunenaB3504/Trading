Indian prices do not move alone. Three outside forces show up again and again.

**The dollar and the rupee.** A weaker rupee makes imports such as oil dearer and helps exporters earn more rupees for the same dollars. Foreign investors who sell Indian shares must also sell rupees for dollars, which pushes the rupee down. The USD/INR practice scenarios show this link.

**Interest rates.** When rates rise, bond prices fall and fixed deposits look better compared with shares. Shares that depend on borrowing, such as banks and real estate, often react most. Look at the bond yield before you judge a share price as cheap.

**Commodity prices.** Oil and metals change the costs and the earnings of many companies. A rise in oil is a cost for an airline and a gain for an oil producer.

**What to do with this.** These forces explain why a good company's shares fall when nothing is wrong with the company. They do not tell you the next move. Compare what you own with what outside changes would do to it, then size the position so that a wrong guess does not hurt.

**Habit.** When a price moves sharply, check the rupee, the bond yield and oil before you blame the company.
